# Project instructions

## Responsive design applies to every page

The user requires this page and all future pages to adapt to phones, tablets, touch tablets, flip/foldable devices, dual-screen layouts, Mac/Windows laptops, desktops, and ultrawide displays.

- Design around available width, height, orientation, and input capabilities, not device brands or user-agent sniffing.
- Reuse the usable-panel pattern in `.page-frame`: safe-area insets, container queries, and fluid bounded typography and spacing.
- Keep content and controls out of exposed foldable hinges using viewport-segment features where supported. Ordinary responsive layout is the fallback when segment information is unavailable.
- Keep touch targets at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels. Do not make functionality depend on hover. Support mouse, touch, keyboard, and reduced motion.
- The site is a full-screen, single-viewport sales presentation: the document never scrolls and each page is designed to fit the viewport. Where a short window or zoom cannot fit a page, its `.page-scroll` region scrolls so nothing becomes unreachable. Use safe centering (`justify-center-safe`, `[align-content:safe_center]`) in scrollable regions. Do not disable browser zoom.
- Size decorative canvas scenes to their actual container; map pointer positions relative to canvas bounds. Use lower rendering quality for compact or coarse-pointer devices.
- Keep content readable on narrow split-screen windows and landscape phones, not only standard portrait breakpoints.
- For new pages, check representative sizes: 320x568, 390x844, 844x390, 768x1024, 1024x768, 1440x900, 1920x1080, and 2560x1080. Include narrow foldable panels, orientation changes, 200% zoom, keyboard focus, and touch behavior.
- Run the production build after code changes. Report visual/device checks honestly; never claim every physical device was tested from a build alone.

## Presentation shell

- The app only runs in full screen. `src/app/FullscreenGate.jsx` covers the app until the visitor taps; leaving full screen brings the gate back and keeps the route. Browsers without the Fullscreen API (iPhone Safari) get a one-time start screen instead. `?fullscreen=off` bypasses the gate in development builds only, for automated screenshots.
- Styling is Tailwind v4 (`src/styles/index.css`). Custom breakpoints: `3xl` 1920px, `4xl` 2560px, `5xl` 3840px; the root font size also steps up at each. Layout variants: `split` (two columns on laptops and on landscape phones), `stack` (portrait phones and tablets), `short` (height ≤ 544px). Use tone-aware colours (`text-fg`, `text-muted`, `border-line`, `text-accent`) and set `data-tone="light"` on cream grounds.
- New pages: add the route to `src/app/routes.js` and `src/app/TransitionStage.jsx`, choose a scene preset in `src/scenes/sceneStore.js`, and mark arriving elements with `data-reveal` (see `src/app/reveal.js`).
- GSAP with React StrictMode: wrap effect animations in `gsap.context()` and `revert()` it on cleanup; never `tween.kill()` a `from()` tween. Do not give GSAP-animated elements CSS transitions on `transform`, `opacity` or `filter`; animate hover nudges with the separate `translate` property instead.
- Typography: the brochure's own fonts are served from `public/fonts` (see `src/styles/brand-fonts.css`): Billie Eilish for headlines, Gotham Book/Medium for text, labels and figures, Avenir LT Std Black for the lockup, Swiss 721 Condensed for map labels, Myriad Pro for the north arrow. No other typefaces, and no italic serif. Put every number in `.num` (upright, lining, tabular); the Billie Eilish file has no usable figures (1 is blank, 5, 6 and 8 are missing); never set figures in italic. Separate an index from its label with space, not long dash rules.
- Restraint: the presentation is minimal and premium, not flashy. The silk veil is the only decorative 3D element, and it appears only on Home and the Neu Gen gilded chapter (brochure page 2); every other page sits on a still gradient preset. The one other 3D element is the tower's architectural model (`src/scenes/building/BuildingModel.jsx`, `public/models/ascend-block.glb`), on Residences (display, and Visual selection, where a tower and one of its floors are picked to open their plans; each tower is selected separately), and its lighter copy standing on its site on the Location map (`public/models/ascend-map.glb`, see Location). Wherever it appears, show `ModelNote`: the model is a temporary representation to be replaced by the final, fully coloured model. It loads lazily, has its own on-demand canvas and draws nothing when still. Floor data lives in `buildingModel` in `src/content/template.js` and `src/scenes/building/floors.js` (no three.js there, so pages can import it). The Tower page's orbit is drone footage cut into 360 frames (`src/pages/tower/OrbitViewer.jsx`, `public/orbit/`), drawn to a 2D canvas only while it moves, with frames fetched coarse to fine and only those near the view decoded. The Views page's 360° photo panoramas (Marzipano, loaded with the page, drawing only while the view moves) are photographs, not models. They turn slowly by default (off under reduced motion, paused behind the menu) with a stop button; the three times of day are layers of one scene on one shared view, so a time change is an in-place crossfade even mid-drag or mid-spin. Replacement panoramas must be re-projected onto the evening shot's frame first (`public/asset-notes.md`). Do not add other WebGL models. The Location map's weather (haze, clouds seen from above, drifting cloud shadows, sun shadows, dusk light) is the one atmosphere: keep it soft, slow and clear of what the map frames. No glitter, particles, bloom, spinning lights, pulsing or pinging elements, glows, blur transitions or elastic/overshoot easing. Motion is short fades and small rises (about 1 s or less); interactive elements change colour or shift a few pixels. The exceptions are the brand line art that draws in with GSAP DrawSVG (`drawIn()` in `src/app/reveal.js`) and rests as the plain filled artwork: Home's Arkade Ascend lockup (`src/components/AscendLockup.jsx`, `data-reveal="draw"`) and the brochure's NEU GEN monogram (`src/components/NeuGenMark.jsx`, `data-ch="draw"` on the Neu Gen cover and welcome card; also the still watermark of *want it all*).
- Drone views of the site (`public/assets/Tower A`, `Tower B`, `Top View`: Marzipano cube-map exports, listed in `src/content/panoramas.js`) open full screen from "See view" on Residences' visual selection, from Floor Plans and from a plan card or home in the unit finder: `src/components/ViewOverlay.jsx` (a dialog portalled into the page frame, like the full-screen plan) over `PanoramaViewer.jsx` (the Views page's viewer, one layer per floor or time so a change crossfades in place). Each wing was flown at the height of 11 floors (5, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 37); a chosen floor shows the nearest one. They are photographs, labelled as the site at that height, not the residence's own view.
- Without WebGL the silk falls back to a still plum ground and Views to still images; the presentation must keep working.
- The shared canvas renders on demand (`sceneStore.dirty`, set by `setScenePreset`): continuously only while the silk is on screen or a mood is blending. Still pages stay at zero draw calls.
- Location uses MapLibre like our other projects' maps (Zenith, Hariko): OpenFreeMap's Positron style recoloured to the brochure palette, extruded 3D buildings, all one solid warm brown with no per-building colours (this source's tiles repeat feature ids, so data-driven colours paint whole blocks; the pin marks the project; OpenStreetMap heights above 200 m are tagging errors and are drawn low-rise), a camera that stays close to the tower (`src/pages/location/shots.js`, which models MapLibre's perspective to place it), flown with GSAP, road routes from OSRM (an arc if unavailable), and a 3D / 2D / brochure-map switch. The map fills the screen: the switch sits in the header row beside the logo (below the header on phones), and the location panel floats over the map (right on laptops and landscape phones, a bottom sheet on portrait screens). The map's camera padding equals whatever covers it, so routes are framed, and the 3D perspective centred, in the open part; zoom and attribution sit bottom left (zoom buttons hidden on phones, which pinch). It loads lazily with the page. Keep the OpenStreetMap attribution visible. Offline, the page falls back to the brochure map. Live-map positions are indicative and labelled so.
  - The tower stands on its site in a three.js custom layer (`src/pages/location/towerLayer.js`) that shares the map's camera, depth buffer and light; the site's own OpenStreetMap buildings are cut out of the tiles (`siteTiles.js`). Keep it the map's top layer: MapLibre draws flat layers above its first 3D layer without a depth test, so the route must be drawn before it. It rises with the city from zoom 13.5, folds into a flat plan in 2D, carries the project's label on its top, frames itself when tapped, and shows `ModelNote` whenever it is on screen. The camera's shots: the tower's front (`HERO`), by default and when it is tapped; and for a place, from the tower looking out, the tower large on the left and the route running from its foot to the place on the right, tilted up towards the horizon only as far as the place needs (on a short opening, a far place draws the camera back instead). A beat after the place is in view (`OUT`, `HOLD`, `RISE` in `LiveMap.jsx`, about 3.5 s in all: visitors move quickly between places) the camera rises (`aerial()`, 42 degrees, same direction) until the whole route is in view, so the distance shows; a resize or reduced motion goes straight to that view, and a new choice or a drag cancels the rise. Flights between shots hold the tower where it stands on screen, so the camera circles it; the arrival spirals down onto it. Shots never bring the camera below 210 m once the horizon is in view: the sky is drawn over everything below it, so no city building may rise past the horizon. 2D keeps the whole route fitted, straight down. Site, footprints and placement are `buildingModel.map` in `template.js`.
  - One sun, fixed to the compass (`SUN`, `DUSK_SUN` in `LiveMap.jsx`), lights the city and the tower and casts the tower's shadow (a depth map redrawn only when the sun or the tower's height changes). The same layer draws the weather: haze to the horizon, a cloud deck 1.8 to 2.6 km up that shows only when zoomed out and parts wherever it would cover the framed journey or the tower (`focus()`), and the clouds' shadows drifting over the ground. The visit's first arrival comes down through the clouds. The Dusk switch in the panel blends every colour in `PALETTE`, the sun, the sky, the clouds and the tower's lit windows over 1.4 s.
  - The wind redraws a still map at 15 frames a second (10 on touch screens) and never under reduced motion, behind the menu, while the page leaves or the tab is hidden. For those frames labels are placed without their fade (MapLibre's private `_fadeDuration`; any camera move restores it), otherwise each gust becomes 300 ms of continuous drawing. Quality (`QUALITY` in `towerLayer.js`): fewer cloud levels on touch and small screens (drawn at most at 2× pixel ratio), no clouds where the device saves data or has little memory or few cores, and a first flight slower than 22 frames a second lowers it. Without WebGL 2 the map runs without the tower and weather; after a lost WebGL context the tower is added again.
- Animation ownership (prevents overlapping or half-finished animations):
  - Each animated property of an element has one owner. An entrance tween and an interactive tween must not both drive, say, `scale`; animate a proxy value or a wrapper instead.
  - Interactive tweens that can retrigger use `overwrite: true` or `'auto'`. Sequenced player timelines (chapters, carousels) kill the previous run and settle other states before starting.
  - Route changes: the stage stops the page's entrance before its exit, and the exit fades the whole page, not only `data-reveal` items. Navigation from the menu (`location.state.instant`) swaps pages before paint, and the menu closes in the same commit as the new location. React Router renders navigations as transitions, so never close overlays in a separate urgent update.
  - Page-owned 3D (the building model) sits inside its page, so it fades with the page's exit and with the menu. Any other page-owned 3D reads `sceneStore.leaving` and `sceneStore.menuOpen` and fades with the page. Its one-time entrance runs only when its page mounts.
- Content that is not in the brochure lives in `src/content/template.js` and is labelled "indicative" on screen. Never present template content as confirmed project fact.
- The current brochure is `Arkade Ascend-Brochure - 24-9-2026.pdf`. Its facts (Neu Gen welcome copy, specifications, Club Ark amenities, nearby places, connectivity) live in `src/content/project.js`; its photographs are in `public/brochure/` (`specs/`, `amenities/`, `welcome-balcony-*`). The brochure marks them representational (the tower as a dummy render, the kitchen as an artist impression): keep those notes on screen.
- The unit finder (Residences) and Floor Plans read one inventory, `src/content/inventory.js`, built from Wing A's plan sheets in `src/content/floorPlans.js` (supplied 8 Oct 2026; areas and room sizes transcribed as printed, drawings trimmed into `public/plans/wing-a/`). One typical floor plan serves every Wing A floor (1 to 37, all with homes for now), with six units; floor 7 and floor 36 have their own Unit 05 (`floorExceptions`), and floor 36 has no Unit 06 because its Unit 05 takes that space. Floor Plans opens on the typical plan with each unit's outline traced on it (`typicalPlan.zones`), then a unit's own drawing and room schedule. Wing B has no plans yet: it stays out of the inventory, and its floors show a "plans to follow" state until its sheets are supplied.
